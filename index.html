About PADL

I started PADL because of a feeling I could not shake. Looking at how universities around the world teach, I saw lectures recorded, libraries digitised, and notes, books and practice problems available to students at any hour. Then I looked at SSN, and I felt we were missing out. Our library had not been digitised, and a great deal of knowledge was still tied to a shelf, a timetable and a room.

I did not want to wait for someone else to close that gap, so I decided to start closing it myself. PADL is my attempt to bring SSN level with the global higher-education market, with course material that lives online and is ready whenever a student is.

Students should be the head of their own learning.

Why asynchronous

"Asynchronous" is the heart of the idea. There is no fixed class hour. You can read a lesson on the bus, attempt the practice questions at midnight, and come back to a hard idea as many times as you need. The pace, the order and the effort are yours to decide.

Where the inspiration came from

Two platforms shaped how I think about this. Khan Academy showed me how clear, short lessons paired with practice can make an idea click. NPTEL showed me that rigorous, university-grade teaching can be open to every engineering student in India. PADL borrows from both and tailors the experience to SSN: examples from our own classrooms, labs and campus life, and courses built around what our students need next.
